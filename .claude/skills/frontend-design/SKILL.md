# Frontend Design Skill Summary

This resource guides designers in creating distinctive, intentional visual interfaces rather than templated defaults. The core philosophy treats design as a deliberate practice grounded in subject matter.

## Key Principles

**Foundation in Subject**: Before designing, identify the concrete subject, audience, and primary function. The subject's own materials and vernacular should inform distinctive choices.

**Typography as Personality**: "Pair the display and body faces deliberately, not the same families you would reach for on any other project" with intentional scale, weights, and spacing.

**Structure Encodes Meaning**: Layout choices should communicate information, not merely decorate. Numbered sequences are only appropriate when order actually matters to understanding.

**Deliberate Motion**: Animation should serve the subject—not scattered effects that suggest AI generation. Consider where motion genuinely enhances the experience.

## Process

Work in two passes: first develop a compact design plan (color palette, typography, layout concept, signature element), then critique it against the brief to ensure it's specific to *this* project rather than a generic default. Only build after confirming the plan's uniqueness.

## Quality Standards

Spend boldness in one distinctive element; keep surroundings quiet. Build baseline quality (responsive design, keyboard accessibility, reduced-motion support) without fanfare. Copy should clarify understanding through active voice and plain language, never apologize for errors, and maintain consistent vocabulary throughout.
